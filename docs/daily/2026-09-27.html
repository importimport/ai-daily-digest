📄 Papers

Learning to Discover Interesting Mathematics
The paper defines a theorem's intrinsic interestingness as the ratio of proof length to statement length, pushing LLMs beyond proving theorems toward finding ones worth proving.
Your Transformer Can Hold Two Thoughts at Once
The authors propose a Superposition Linearity Hypothesis, showing that linearly combining two text streams yields a superposition of next-token distributions, an intrinsic property of the architecture rather than a training artifact.
Rufus-Air: An Open LLM Post-Training Recipe
A fully open post-training recipe on GLM-4.5-Air-Base (106B-A12B) with eight stages: SFT, Reasoning RL, Coding RL, Instruction-Following RL, General/Coding/Search Agent, and RLHF.
AgentKernel: The Trust-Native Agentic Operating System
The paper argues agents need OS-level security boundaries rather than application-level middleware sharing a process trust domain with the agents it monitors.
Training Object Permanence in World Models
The authors introduce the WROP dataset and training method to test and improve object permanence and solidity priors in video world models.
ExplorationBench: Measuring AI Systems' Exploration in Verifiable Alien Worlds
A benchmark that evaluates AI exploration in verifiable alien environments, separating genuine discovery from recall of pretraining data.
Capable yet Parsimonious: Extracting Hidden Chain-of-Thought in Frontier Models
By registering a simple custom tool, the authors induce closed frontier models to externalize intermediate reasoning, validated against native CoT on open models.
Calibration as a First-Class Criterion in LLM Evaluation
The paper argues calibration (agreement between confidence and correctness) is systematically ignored in mainstream LLM evaluation and should be a first-class metric.
