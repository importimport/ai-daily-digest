### Today's Take

The clearest signal today is that AI competition is shifting from "model capability" to the infrastructure and trust layers around models. In a single day OpenAI shipped Images 2.6, acquired Pierre, and unveiled an automated kernel-vulnerability patching pipeline, while Meta picked up Conductor — all filling in pieces around agent workflows rather than launching new base models. Research echoes this: FlashProve uses cryptography to prove "this response really came from this model," and the Stanford study shows nearly half of LLM findings have expired — both pointing at a long-neglected problem: verifiability of model behavior. Another line worth watching is physical constraints biting back: 30-month PCB lead times and data centers losing across the midterm ballot, making the political and supply-chain costs of compute expansion a hard ceiling. For builders, the two most practical moves right now: seriously evaluate context compaction (long context may be overrated), and treat agent memory and observability as first-class design concerns.
